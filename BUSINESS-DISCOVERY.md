# Kanasu Gari — business discovery

Research date: 10 November 2026. Planning only; implementation has not started.

## Evidence and limits

The owner identifies Kanasu Gari as a handmade crochet business.

The [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@kanasu_gari) describes handmade and customized products. Retrieved titles include crochet keychains, cushion covers, roses, a bunny, yarn sourcing and a tassel dreamcatcher making video. Channel keywords also mention accessories, scrunchies, wall hangings, amigurumi and earrings; these keywords alone do not establish current stock.

Direct Instagram scraping was unsupported. A structured public-post provider returned a sample of 12 posts for [@kanasu_gari](https://www.instagram.com/kanasu_gari/). Captions provide evidence of:

This was a caption/title review, not a complete catalogue or visual brand audit. No product prices, delivery promises, maker biography or full ordering policies were established. A cotton-yarn mention applies to one toy post, not every product. Product safety, material composition and suitability must be confirmed per item before publishing claims. Social engagement does not establish sales or bestsellers.

## Working business interpretation

Kanasu Gari combines handmade crochet objects, customization and creator-led storytelling. Its visible range spans home decoration, gifts, accessories and crochet characters. Both ready-made and custom work appear relevant, but the owner needs to confirm the current split.

Potential customer motivations, inferred from product types: a personal gift, home decoration, festive decoration or an accessory with handmade character. These are hypotheses, not verified customer demographics.

The website should help someone discover a piece, understand its scale/materials and customization options, and confidently start an order. Showing the making process can explain the work behind the price and lead time.

## Proposed information structure — subject to owner input

1. Hero featuring a real signature piece and a concise brand promise.
2. Browse collections: home and festive decor, flowers, accessories, crochet characters. Final categories follow the active catalogue.
3. Selected creations with dimensions, materials, availability, price or quotation basis and care information.
4. Custom orders: choose a product, describe colors/size/occasion, provide a reference if needed, then request feasibility, quote and timeline.
5. Meet the maker and watch the craft process.
6. Customer stories, only when real reviews and permission are available.
7. Ordering, delivery and care FAQs; social/contact links.

An enquiry-led first release fits the observed DM ordering behavior. Direct Instagram enquiry is already supported by evidence; WhatsApp and checkout remain undecided.

## Interaction opportunities — design hypotheses

- A yarn line draws between sections as visitors scroll.
- A short yarn-to-finished-piece sequence introduces the craft.
- Draggable product cards use real product cutouts and subtle depth.
- A color-selection preview for eligible custom pieces uses owner-approved options; distinguish illustrative previews from guaranteed finished colors.
- Hanging decor responds gently to pointer movement.
- Process clips open on demand, with mobile and reduced-motion alternatives.

The saree reference supplies useful carousel, motion and component patterns. Its silk physics does not model crochet stitches or plush construction. Accurate crochet 3D would require suitable models/textures or a separate modeling workflow; real photography is the strongest initial product representation.

No colors, fonts or logo treatment have been selected yet. A warm, tactile direction is a proposal, not an observed brand identity.

## Questions before design

1. Which product families and 5–10 signature pieces should lead the business now?
2. What is ready to ship versus made to order, what can be customized, and what are typical prices and making times?
3. Should the first website accept payments or send an enquiry through Instagram/WhatsApp?
4. Where does the business ship, and what delivery/payment/return policies already exist?
5. What is the maker's story, the meaning of the name, and which existing logo, colors and product photos should guide the visual identity?

Next milestone: confirm the business brief, then develop page flow and visual design concepts before writing application code.
